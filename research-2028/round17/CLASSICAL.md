# The classical security reduction for the standalone KEM

The same encapsulation protocol admits a tighter proof when all oracle queries are classical. This is the explicit-rejection Fujisaki–Okamoto transform with a hash of the message as the shared key. Its tight classical relationship to chosen-plaintext security is established in [Hofheinz, Hövelmanns and Kiltz, TCC 2017](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/604). Below we specialize the argument to one SG-DIFRA recipient, using the repository's raw-gap convention and the actual tape and key-generation bounds. The [quantum proof](../round16/MATCHED_PROOF.md) uses a different simulation.

## Statement

Let $\epsilon$ bound the raw two-message IND-CPA gap of the finite-tape randomized public-key encryption with exactly uniform private checks. The budget must cover the simulator below. Write $\ell$ for the number of message bits, $\kappa$ for the secret seed bits, $s_S$ for private support sampler slack and $t$ for the number of checks. Let $2^{-\gamma}$ bound the probability of each individual randomized ciphertext given any fixed honest key and message. Let $\delta$ bound the probability over a fresh tape that private decryption fails to recover any fixed message on each honest key. Both the [shorter layout](outer_proof.md) and its [quotient encoding](QUOTIENT.md) have this per-key guarantee. The full-radius versions have $\delta=0$.

Count public calls to the two target hash domains by $q_G,q_H$ and set $q=q_G+q_H$. Count secret-expansion oracle calls by $q_S$ and allowed classical decapsulations by $q_D$. In the challenge-at-start game, the raw real-key versus random-key gap obeys

$$
\Delta_{\rm cl}\le\min\{1,\ \epsilon+q2^{-\ell}+2q_D2^{-\gamma}+2q_G\delta+2q_S2^{-\kappa}+2t2^{-s_S}\}.
$$

For adaptive prechallenge decapsulations add $2q_{\rm pre}2^{-\ell}+2\delta$ when $q_{\rm pre}>0$. The guessing success for a fair challenge bit is at most $1/2+\Delta_{\rm cl}/2$. Setting $q_D=q_{\rm pre}=0$ gives IND-CPA. A simulator can store a dictionary of generated ciphertexts and keys, using at most one native base encryption for each distinct queried coin-hash address at that recipient. Charge its running time, memory and independent random-oracle simulation to $\epsilon$.

## Proof

1. First analyze independent exact private checks. Preselect the challenge message $M$ uniformly from all $2^\ell$ strings, draw its full encryption tape independently, and reserve that value for $G(M)$. In the real-key experiment reserve the challenge key for $H(M)$. In the comparison experiment the challenge key is independently uniform. The two experiments coincide up to the first public query to $G$ or $H$ on $M$, provided the decapsulation simulation agrees. Only target-suite, target-public-key queries matter here. Other oracle slices are independent. Hash queries revealing a prefix of the same oracle output count as touching that address.
2. In a classical simulation log every public query that reveals $G(m)$. Form its canonical ciphertext and store the message indexed by those bytes. On a permitted decapsulation return $H(m)$ on a dictionary match and reject otherwise. For each fresh $m\ne M$ queried before the first target hit, $G(m)$ has fresh uniform coins. For a fixed key its encryption fails to decrypt to $m$ with probability at most $\delta$, even for adaptively selected $m$. With probability at least $1-q_G\delta$ all cached encryptions decrypt to their respective messages. On this event a cache entry cannot conflict with another cached message. This includes entries whose key-hash answer the simulator computes itself.
3. The simulated transcript gives no information about a coin-hash value at an unseen $m\ne M$. Fix the private key, the other relevant oracle values and the simulated transcript leading to a submitted ciphertext $c$. Base decryption determines a candidate message. For an unseen candidate other than $M$, the probability its fresh tape reproduces exactly $c$ is at most $2^{-\gamma}$. For candidate $M$, re-encryption gives the forbidden challenge ciphertext. Bound the union of discrepancies along the simulated transcript, which always rejects unseen entries. This avoids conditioning on what a series of genuine rejections may reveal. Summing over both challenge worlds costs at most $2q_D2^{-\gamma}+2q_G\delta$ before the target hit.
4. To bound the hit, use the randomized base IND-CPA game. Pick independent uniform messages $M_0,M_1$ and use the base challenge ciphertext and an independent uniform challenge key. Simulate using fresh hash tables and stop when a query touches $M_0$. When the base challenge encrypts $M_0$, the view up to this stopping point has the required distribution. No query has read the reserved $G(M_0)$ or $H(M_0)$. When the challenge encrypts $M_1$, the simulation without the stopping rule is independent of $M_0$. Its list of at most $q$ addresses contains $M_0$ with probability at most $q/2^\ell$. The difference in the hit probabilities is bounded by one raw base gap $\epsilon$. The reduction knows $M_0$ and checks every address. With the simulated decapsulator, a single coupling of the two key experiments thus bounds their raw gap by $\epsilon+q2^{-\ell}$.
5. To cover prechallenge queries, initially hide the independently sampled challenge and couple to a game that rejects it throughout. Fix the entire oracle and the keys. Among messages whose deterministic FO encryptions decrypt correctly, different messages have different ciphertexts. At most $q_{\rm pre}$ good messages map into a fixed prechallenge list. The expected fraction of bad messages is at most $\delta$ for the independently sampled oracle. The two worlds together cost the additional term above. Finally expand a uniform private seed as in the [secret-seed hybrid](../round14/PRIVATE_SEED.md). With a fresh independent tape substituted, the secret address is hidden and a classical observer touches it with probability at most $q_S/2^\kappa$. In either world, switching that tape to exact check sampling costs at most $t2^{-s_S}$. This gives the displayed bound.

## The 192 example

At the original full-radius dimensions, $\epsilon\le C\eta_{\rm cl}+U$, where $C<10{,}516$ and $U<2^{-318}$. Here $\eta_{\rm cl}$ bounds the classical raw common-syndrome distinguishing gap at the composed resources. The shorter layout decreases $C$, preserves that upper bound on $U$ and has $\delta<2^{-607}$. For the suites retaining at least 896 bits of spread, $\ell=\kappa=512$ and $s_S=576$. Bound each of $q,q_D,q_S$ by $2^{191}$. Substitution yields the following sufficient conditions, including the two-stage terms.

| Hypothetical bound on $\eta_{\rm cl}$ | Classical KEM guessing success |
|---|---|
| $2^{-20}$ | Below 50.8% |
| $2^{-47}$ | Below $1/2+2^{-34}$ |

Both sufficient conditions also hold for the primary [systematic half-radius layout](SYSTEMATIC.md). Conditioning on the invertibility of the leading block in its reduction costs a factor below two. Its coefficient is below 11,816 and its additive base term is below $2^{-316}$. Its certified spread is 901 bits. The [exact rational checks](test_classical.py) verify the two classical claims and the two QROM examples at the more conservative systematic constants. The actual value of $\eta_{\rm cl}$ at the simulated resources remains unknown. Superposition queries cannot be logged this way. The [QROM analysis](../round16/MATCHED_PROOF.md) supplies the post-quantum conditional claim.
