# What the earlier literature already establishes

The candidate contribution of Round 10 is a restriction theorem. The published map is an explicitly listed vector of ternary polynomials. The same two local response coordinates see separate addresses across all challenges. Additional coordinates may see the complete challenge. In this model the quadratic problem is PSPACE-complete, and one square-free cubic monomial in the entire map suffices for NEXP-completeness. Each component of the hard family has at most five monomials. The cubic construction also has a single linear term. Deleting either the cubic term or the linear term separately gives a winning policy on every constructed instance. After deletion of the linear term, the zero response works on every challenge. These are statements about the specified representation and constructions. We found close precedents for each of the underlying mechanisms.

## The closest Boolean construction

Chen, Huang, Lu and Tan reduce circuit-specified graph 3-colourability to dependency quantified Boolean formulas (DQBF). Section III.A already uses two local vertex addresses. Each side returns two Boolean values encoding one colour. Equal addresses must give equal colours, and the endpoints of an edge must give different colours. This is precisely the consistency argument used by the cubic reduction here. Its use as a source of NEXP-hardness predates Round 10. [Chen et al., FMCAD 2022, §III.A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06014)

A ternary coordinate can hold one of three colours directly. The remaining work is to verify a circuit-specified edge using sparse *directional differences*, and to account for every term introduced by that verifier. A public challenge includes a proposed trace of the adjacency circuit. The quadratic guard rows pass a quantity through successive local tests. Incorrect traces cannot activate the edge condition. For colours with difference $D$, the two field elements $D-1$ and $D+1$ have product zero exactly when the colours differ. Two distinct stationary helper coordinates store these factors as $M,N$. The clock variable $s$ turns their product into the difference of a single cubic monomial $sMN$. In particular, the polynomial size of a standard Boolean colouring verifier alone would give no bound of one on the number of cubic monomials.

Cheng, Fung, Jiang, Slivovsky and Tan show that a DNF formula with three existential Boolean variables and pairwise disjoint dependencies already gives NEXP-hardness. Their two-variable DNF case is PSPACE-complete, while their disjoint two-variable CNF case is NL-complete. Scholl, Jiang, Wimmer and Ge-Ernst earlier showed a third-level polynomial hierarchy bound, with matching hardness, for CNF formulas where every two dependency sets are identical or disjoint. Both the answer alphabet and the permitted verification predicate matter. The Round 10 compiler also uses full-view helpers, whose dependency set contains both address blocks. Calling this a result for “two Boolean outputs” or “all dependencies disjoint” would omit a hypothesis of the comparison. [Cheng et al., SAT 2025, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2025.10) · [Scholl et al., AAAI 2019](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33011584)

There is also a classical games interpretation. A perfect strategy for the classical graph-colouring game reads a vertex on each side and answers with a colour. The connection between two noncommunicating provers and nondeterministic exponential time extends much further. Feige and Lovász characterized the power of two-prover one-round interactive proofs. Round 10 asks for deterministic tables satisfying every challenge of one explicit algebraic verifier. The problem definition specifies neither an approximation gap for failed strategies nor quantum measurements. [Cameron et al., *On the quantum chromatic number of a graph*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0608016) · [Feige and Lovász, STOC 1992](https://research.ibm.com/publications/two-prover-one-round-proof-systems-their-power-and-their-problems)

## Prior algebraic formulations

Ding and Tan study functions for which the number of solutions of $f(x+a)-f(x)=0$ is constant over nonzero directions. Their definition establishes the collision equation's history. Here the input is a compact polynomial map, the directions form a prescribed family, and the same partially informed tables must furnish all the responses. [Ding and Tan, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/15598608.2012.647479)

Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, Genkin and Tromer give a particularly relevant algebraic precedent. Their succinct algebraic constraint satisfaction problem asks for a univariate assignment polynomial over a characteristic-two extension field. At each point of a specified subspace, the values of this assignment on affine neighbours must satisfy a polynomial constraint. Section 9 uses reductions from succinct circuit satisfiability. Earlier univariate algebraic CSP constructions likewise encoded bounded computations through algebraic graph colouring. The general idea of verifying an exponentially large labelling through local finite-field equations is well established. The parameters tracked here are the degree and monomial counts of a *published potential over the fixed field* $\mathbb F_3$ and the observations of its responder coordinates. The response tables themselves have no imposed algebraic degree bound. [Ben-Sasson et al., *On the Concrete-Efficiency Threshold of Probabilistically-Checkable Proofs*, §§7, 9](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2012/045/revision/1/download/) · [Earlier univariate algebraic CSP, Definition 6.1 and Theorem 6.2](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2005/bghsv2-full.pdf)

Kayal discusses ordinary quantified polynomial systems over finite fields and records PSPACE-completeness when the quantifier sequence and number of variables may grow. His bounded-variable question allows the field to vary. Round 10 fixes the field and allows the address length to grow. The two private response coordinates restrict the information available to functions whose tables may contain exponentially many entries. This distinction matters when comparing variable counts. [Kayal, doctoral thesis, 2007, §8.4](https://www.cse.iitk.ac.in/users/manindra/Students/thesis_kayal.pdf)

## What the older matrix theorem gives for Round 9

Buss, Frandsen and Shallit prove that deciding whether a parameterized square matrix admits a singular specialization is NP-complete over each fixed finite field. Their input already allows a variable to occur in several entries. Write one of their matrices over $\mathbb F_3$ as $A(c)=A_0+\sum_i c_iA_i$. Its complement asks whether every specialization is invertible. For a square matrix this is equivalent to asking whether, for all $c,d$, some $y$ satisfies $A(c)y=d$. [Buss et al., BRICS RS-96-33, Corollary 10, p. 19](https://www.brics.dk/RS/96/33/BRICS-RS-96-33.pdf)

The following translation into Round 9's notation is our deduction from that corollary. Set

$$
 P(s,x,t,y)=\left(sA_0+\sum_i x_iA_i\right)y-t,
 \qquad h=(1,c,d,0).
$$

For every base point, $P(z+h)-P(z)=A(c)y-d$. The quadratic map is polynomial in the matrix description size. Thus the earlier matrix theorem directly supplies Round 9's coNP-hardness for quadratic, pointwise challenge coverage. That reduction lets $y$ depend on the entire challenge. The Round 10 quadratic lower bound instead uses shared labels on a circuit-specified graph, forcing equality along paths between pinned endpoints. The underlying source, succinct undirected accessibility, was proved PSPACE-hard by Balcázar, Lozano and Torán. For the upper bound, explicitly expanding all table entries produces a system of linear equations over $\mathbb F_3$. The existing modular-logspace algorithm for explicit linear feasibility is one way to process this succinct matrix in polynomial space. [Balcázar et al., 1992, Theorem 38](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/succinct.pdf) · [de Beaudrap, *On the complexity of solving linear congruences and computing nullspaces modulo a constant*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3949)

## What the minimum-challenge result measures

Exponential phenomena in universal expansion are established. For example, Beyersdorff, Blinkhorn and Peitl prove exponential separations between particular DQBF resolution systems equipped with different dependency schemes. Our path calculation counts a different object. For the symmetric graph construction it identifies the *smallest number of complete challenges* whose simultaneous collision requirements contradict one another. It equals the smallest odd integer at least the graph distance between the two pins. A path through all binary addresses of width $n$ gives exactly $2^n-1$. A short symbolic description of this path can coexist with a long explicit refuting list. [Beyersdorff et al., 2020](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2020/036/) · [Round 10 proposition and proof](OBSTRUCTION.md)

In the complementary two-view bound, agreement between the two response groups defines a polynomial affine in each address block separately. Interpolating such a polynomial from rows and columns uses established algebra. Row-versus-column agreement also has a long history in the testing of tensor-product codes. The question studied here is exact feasibility with arbitrary local response tables, followed by a dimension argument to extract an explicit inconsistent list of complete challenges. The no-helper polynomial list bound and the path family together give an unconditional separation in the size of that particular certificate. [Chiesa, Manohar and Shinkar, 2020](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v016a005/) · [Two-view theorem and explicit bound](TWO_VIEW_BOUND.md)

## Search scope and priority

On **18 November 2026**, targeted web searches and an arXiv full-text search covered dependency quantified Boolean formulas with disjoint observations, succinct 3-colouring and undirected connectivity, one-round two-prover games, succinct algebraic CSP, quantified polynomial equations over finite fields, zero-difference functions, modular linear feasibility, row and column interpolation, and lower bounds for universal expansion. Searches for equivalents included combinations of `finite differences`, `Skolem functions`, `one cubic monomial`, `ternary`, `succinct graph colouring`, `local strategies`, and `PSPACE NEXP`. The sources above identify the constructions and theorem statements that overlap substantively.

This search located no earlier statement of the combined fixed-field degree classification with the displayed two-trit, one-square-free-cubic construction and single-linear-term deletion property, or of the exact challenge count for this compiler. A differently named equivalent or an immediate consequence of another published normal form remains possible. The contribution offered for review consists of those precise restrictions and proofs. Worldwide priority and independent peer review remain open.
