# Which differential collision does the security reduction find?

Fix a public ternary matrix $H$ with $r$ rows and $N$ columns and a weight $w$. Draw a uniform signed weight-$w$ vector $e$ and publish the syndrome $y=He$. The search task accepts **any** vector of the prescribed weight and syndrome. Both average-case problems in the security argument have this form, with different dimensions. The [quantum witness theorem](WITNESS_PROOF.md) relates distinguishing to recovery in these experiments. Here we describe their exact representation in the earlier SG-DIFRA mathematics.

Build the public ternary circuit $T_H(e)=(He,\operatorname{bin}(\operatorname{wt}(e)))$. The binary count is least-significant bit first with $\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor+1$ bits. The square of each input trit is its indicator of being nonzero. Starting from a zero counter, add each indicator. For an old bit $b$ and carry $c$, the outgoing carry is $bc$ and the new bit is $b+c+bc$ in the ternary field. Their four possible Boolean input pairs verify this identity. The counter reaches at most $N$ and cannot overflow. Counting only modulo three would identify weight zero with weight three.

Compile this circuit using the [Round 12 pinning construction](../round12/differential_bridge.py). Attach a transparent inert label containing the dimensions and entries of $H$. The challenge is $(y,\operatorname{bin}(w))$. The compiler emits one sparse potential vector of degree at most three and the prescribed direction begins with one. For any supplied input $e$, all auxiliary gate values are uniquely determined. The compiler forces the clock and target coordinates of the starting point to zero, every gate equation to hold, and the output wires to equal the challenge. It follows in both directions that the valid basepoints are exactly $(0,0^{|c|},e,\mathrm{gates}(e))$ for the desired sparse witnesses. Encoding, reading back the tagged public instance, lifting a witness, and projecting a witness take polynomial time. The count circuit has $O(N\log(N+1)+r)$ gates and $O(rN+N\log(N+1))$ wire terms. The tag adds $O(rN+\log(rN+1))$ output components.

Consequently a solver for this generated fixed-challenge differential search distribution solves the matching syndrome search distribution with the same probability and polynomial overhead, and conversely. The reduction from a KEM distinguisher may select either of the two generated distributions. A uniform comparison syndrome may also have a weight-$w$ witness. The distinguishing experiment compares two sampling laws, independently of that fact.

This identifies which part of SG-DIFRA a confidentiality proof can reach. A single planted challenge is given and a witness is requested. Round 9 universal coverage and Round 10 distributed-strategy existence quantify over many challenges and are different decision problems. Their worst-case completeness theorems supply no average-case bound for this distribution. The matrix label is part of a proof encoding. The wire protocol never transmits the expanded circuit or the label.
